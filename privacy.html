Watson Personal Finance
A personal budgeting app

Privacy policy

Last updated November 7, 2026

Who this covers

Watson Personal Finance is a budgeting app that runs on its developer's own computer. Its developer is its only user, and the only person whose financial data it holds. It isn't offered to anyone else. If that ever changes, this policy will be updated before anyone else's data is collected.

What it collects

  • From banks, through Plaid, only for accounts the developer connects: account names, types and balances; transactions; credit card and loan details such as balances, due dates and interest rates; and investment holdings and activity.
  • Entered by the developer: accounts and holdings typed in by hand, imported files, categories, budgets, goals, store and gas station rewards points, and notes.
  • About signing in: the public key of each passkey (never the passkey itself, or any face, fingerprint or PIN), signed-in sessions, and a log of sign-ins and sensitive actions.
  • Not collected: bank usernames and passwords. Those are typed into Plaid's own window and never reach the app. The app has no analytics, ads or trackers.

How it's used

Only to show the developer their own finances: spending, budgets, bills, net worth and investments. It isn't used for advertising, credit decisions or anything else.

Where it's kept

  • Only on the developer's own computer, in the app's database on an encrypted drive. The keys that let the app reach each bank through Plaid are encrypted again inside the database (AES-256).
  • Backups are encrypted files (AES-256) kept on that computer and, if set up, in the developer's own cloud storage. They can't be opened without a separate key.
  • The app talks to Plaid only over encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Who can see it

Only the developer. Every page and action in the app needs the developer to be signed in with a passkey. The app isn't on the internet: it runs only on the developer's computer and doesn't accept connections from any other device. The data isn't shared, sold or rented to anyone. It leaves that computer only as requests to Plaid for the developer's own accounts, and as encrypted backups to cloud storage the developer chooses.

Consent

Nothing is collected from a bank until the developer connects it through Plaid Link, which shows what will be shared and asks for agreement first. Plaid's part is covered by Plaid's End User Privacy Policy. Any bank can be disconnected at any time.

Keeping and deleting data

  • Data is kept for as long as its account is in the app.
  • Removing a bank ends Plaid's access straight away. Its accounts, transactions, balances and holdings can be deleted at the same time, or kept as a record.
  • Deleting an account deletes its transactions, balances and holdings with it.
  • Backups are rotated: recent daily copies plus one a month for 12 months, with older ones deleted automatically. Anything deleted is gone from every backup within 12 months.
  • Temporary copies made before an app update are deleted automatically after 30 days.
  • Sign-in records: ended sessions are deleted after 30 days, and the log of sign-ins and sensitive actions after 400 days.

Security

  • Signing in to the app takes a passkey: Windows Hello (face, fingerprint or PIN), a phone or a security key. There are no passwords to steal or reuse.
  • Sessions end after 30 minutes without activity and after 12 hours at most. Connecting a bank and other sensitive actions ask for the passkey again.
  • Who has access is reviewed every 90 days, and any access given to someone else ends by itself on a set date.
  • The computer's drive is encrypted, it has antivirus and automatic updates, and only the developer can sign in to it.
  • A security check runs every month: drive encryption, antivirus, system updates, and known problems in the app's software.
  • Security problems in the app's software are fixed on a set schedule: critical ones within 7 days.
  • The GitHub account where the app's code is kept requires two-step sign-in. No data is stored there.
